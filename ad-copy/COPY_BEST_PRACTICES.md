# Google Ads Copywriting Best Practices & Guidelines

## 🎯 1. Purpose & Business Context
PPC search ad space is limited and highly competitive. To achieve high click-through rates (CTR) and quality scores, ad copy must be compelling, highly relevant to search queries, and optimized with clear calls to action.

This playbook outlines **Copywriting Best Practices** for our Online Fitness Coaching business. It defines our core Unique Selling Propositions (USPs), emotional copy triggers, Dynamic Keyword Insertion (DKI) formatting, and guidelines for maximizing ad extension visibility.

---

## 📋 2. Copywriting Guidelines & Triggers

### A. Core USPs for Fitness Coaching
Highlight these unique benefits in your headlines and descriptions to stand out from automated fitness apps:
*   **Weekly 1-on-1 Video Calls:** Direct face-to-face accountability with a certified coach (not just text messaging).
*   **Integrated Diet & Fitness:** Workout plans and custom nutrition/macro tracking combined in one program.
*   **Dedicated Human Coaches:** Zero automated app bots. Our coaches are real certified trainers who review check-ins weekly.
*   **Custom-Built Programs:** Programs built to fit busy work and travel schedules (hotel gym workouts, bodyweight routines).

### B. Emotional Copy Triggers
B2B and busy professionals convert when you address their core frustrations:
*   **Frustration with Automation:** *Headline:* "Tired of App Message Bots?" / *Description:* "Get real support from a certified trainer who reviews your progress weekly."
*   **Lack of Accountability:** *Headline:* "Accountability That Works" / *Description:* "Stop starting and stopping. Our coaches keep you on track every single day."
*   **Time Constraints:** *Headline:* "Fit for Busy Lifestyles" / *Description:* "Get custom workout routines built to fit your busy professional schedule."

### C. Dynamic Keyword Insertion (DKI)
*   **What is it?** DKI dynamically inserts the user's search query into your ad headline to make your ad look highly relevant.
*   **Syntax:** `{KeyWord:Default Headline Text}`
*   **Formatting Rules:**
    *   Use Title Case (capitalizes the first letter of each word).
    *   Always set a clear default headline in case the search query is too long (over 30 characters).
    *   *Example:* `{KeyWord:Online Fitness Coach}`. If a user searches for "1 on 1 personal trainer", Google displays "1 On 1 Personal Trainer". If their search query is too long (e.g. "best customized personal training for weight loss online"), Google displays the default "Online Fitness Coach".
*   **When to Use:** Use DKI *only* in position-2 headlines of general service campaigns. **Never use DKI in brand campaigns or competitor campaigns** to avoid trademark policy flags.

## 🚫 4. Common Mistakes to Avoid
*   **Overusing DKI:** Placing DKI in multiple headlines. This can result in repetitive or nonsensical headlines (e.g. displaying "Online Trainer Online Personal Trainer" next to each other). Limit DKI to a single headline per ad.
*   **All CAPS Phrasing:** Writing headlines in all caps (e.g., "HIRE A PERSONAL TRAINER TODAY"). This is a policy violation and Google will disapprove the ad.
*   **Clickbait CTA Claims:** Using misleading headlines like "Lose 20lbs in 3 Days". Focus copy on realistic, sustainable fitness transformation (e.g., "Build Sustainable Fitness Habits").
